# Harness interface design

This document records the interface that Harness currently ships. It is a reference for extending the desktop app without changing its visual language. The implementation remains the source of truth: the theme roles in [`apps/nano/src/theme.zig`](apps/nano/src/theme.zig) and the catalog in [`apps/nano/themes.json`](apps/nano/themes.json), the shared settings widgets in [`apps/nano/src/view_settings.zig`](apps/nano/src/view_settings.zig), the view helpers in [`apps/nano/src/view_kit.zig`](apps/nano/src/view_kit.zig), and [`docs/theme-system.md`](docs/theme-system.md).

## Product shape

Harness is a native workspace for coding agents. Its persistent sidebar holds spaces, sessions, navigation, and account status; the main panel holds a conversation, files, terminal, or settings. A compact composer stays close to the transcript. Settings use a quiet left navigation and a centered content column. The interface favors readable work over decorative chrome, and the workspace mode decides how much chrome is on screen (see [Workspace modes](#workspace-modes)).

New installs select **Codegraff Light** and **Codegraff Dark** and follow the system's appearance; Settings → Appearance can fix it to Light or Dark. These are two authored palettes, not color inversions. Users can choose other bundled or imported themes, an independent accent, and a frosted or opaque surface preference. UI components consume semantic theme roles (`theme.Colors`) instead of hard-coded theme colors.

| Main background | `#faf8f3` | `#1c1a17` |
| Sidebar and shell | `#eae5dc` | `#24211d` |

| Accent | `#b57931` | `#d3a464` |

Both Codegraff variants recommend frost, so a new install starts frosted on macOS, the platform where the app draws frost; Windows and Linux, and fullscreen, use opaque surfaces (`apps/nano/src/app_surface.zig`). The **Frosted glass** switch in Settings → Appearance saves an explicit frosted or opaque choice instead (`apps/nano/src/surface_palette.zig`); frost blurs what sits behind the window, tinted from the mapped shell roles, and the composer's writing plane stays opaque over it. In light mode the content plane advances and the warmer sidebar recedes; in dark mode the content plane is darkest and raised controls get lighter. Borders and restrained shadows separate light cards. Accent marks actions, focus, selection, activity, and usage below warning thresholds. Warning, danger, success, diff, syntax, and terminal colors keep their own meanings. The palettes are defined in [`apps/nano/themes.json`](apps/nano/themes.json), and [`protocol/desktop/theme.json`](protocol/desktop/theme.json) pins the roles they resolve to.

## Type, rhythm, and shape

- The interface face is the system font on macOS and Geist elsewhere; Geist Mono is the face for code and terminal surfaces (`apps/nano/src/theme.zig`). Font families are fixed; Settings → Appearance changes the interface text size.
- Settings pages use a centered column no wider than **768 px**, with **24 px** side padding, **32 px** top padding, and **64 px** bottom padding. Titles are **16 px semibold**, subtitles **13 px muted**, and compact row titles/descriptions **13/12 px** (`header`, `subtitle` and `words` in `view_settings.zig`).
- The base spacing steps are **4, 8, 12, and 16 px**. Closely paired labels and descriptions can use a 1 px optical gap.
- Message bubbles use a **16 px** radius, panels and settings cards **12 px**, and small controls **6 px**. Borders are hairlines; they organize the page without turning every item into a box.
- Hover, active, focused, disabled, loading, warning, and error states have distinct treatments. Motion should explain a state change and leave content legible.

## Design rules

These six rules apply to every new or changed surface. Where the desktop client has a shared helper for a rule, the rule names it, so a component never carries its own copy of the value. Rules 1, 3, 5 and 6 are also stated in the Lean models (`apps/nano/lean-proofs/Nano/DesignCanvas.lean`, `HoverStability.lean`), and the Design Canvas audits them. The first five come from Juliette ([@shedsgns](https://x.com/shedsgns/status/2116198444595388434)); the sixth from Michael Filipiuk ([@MichaelFilipiuk](https://x.com/MichaelFilipiuk/status/2115791791568105887)).

1. **Hit areas are bigger than the icon.** A clickable glyph or chip keeps its visible size, and its pointer target grows around it to at least **32 px** (`hitTargetSize` and `hitTargetValid` in `apps/nano/src/ux_rules.zig`). Grow the target with padding cancelled by an equal negative offset, so the visual does not move: each part of the context card's 8 px savings meter has 12 px of padding above and below, offset back by 12 px (`apps/nano/src/view_context.zig`). Neighbouring targets meet in their shared gutter without overlapping.
2. **Numbers that change use tabular figures.** Token counts, savings, percentages, timers, durations, counters, and chart captions set `.tabular = true` on their text style (OpenType `tnum`). Proportional digits make a ticking value shift the text beside it.
3. **Motion starts fast and lands gently.** Use ease-out-quint, `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`: `ui.motion.catalog.ease_out_quint` (`view_kit.zig`) or `easeOutQuint` (`apps/nano/src/tool_rows.zig`; in `tool_ui_state.zig` a tool row's settle wash takes **240 ms** and a fold **140 ms**). Keep enters and fades at **250 ms or less** and a whole multi-part transition under **800 ms**. Linear timing is only for continuous rotation such as spinners. Reduced motion snaps every transition.
4. **Align optically, not mathematically.** A dot, check, spinner, or icon centered on a line box reads high, because the cap height sits below the box's center. Center such glyphs on the letters: move the glyph down, or the text up, by about **1 px** at 11 to 15 px. Where a connector or another drawing is tied to the glyph, move the text instead. Check by measuring a screenshot: the glyph's ink center and the label's cap center should agree within 0.5 px.
5. **Nested corners are concentric.** An element inset inside a rounded surface is rounded at `outer - border - padding`: `concentricInner(outer, border, padding)` in `ux_rules.zig`. Menu rows inside a 12 px card with a 1 px border and a 4 px inset are rounded at **7 px** (`apps/nano/src/view_menus.zig`). When the result is zero or less, the inner element is square.
6. **Use fewer font sizes and weights.** Compact surfaces (cards, popovers, the composer footer) use three sizes and two weights: **13 px** titles, **12 px** body and **11 px** captions, in regular and medium (the context card's `title`, `body` and `caption` in `view_context.zig`). Build hierarchy beyond that with the theme's text colors (`text`, `muted`, `faint`) rather than another size or weight. Settings pages keep their documented 16 / 13 / 12 px scale.

### Savings in the context card

The context card shows what graff kept out of the context as one number and one meter, not a list. The number is the tokens kept out (`Savings.keptOut()`: tool output kept in files plus what compaction freed) at the card's title size, followed by "saved by graff" in the muted color. Below it, a meter with the context bar's segment geometry (62 segments, 4 px wide, 2 px apart, 1.5 px corners, 8 px tall) splits in-context plus saved tokens into three parts: in context now (neutral), tool output (the Files color), and compaction (the summary blue). Every part that holds anything keeps at least one segment. One caption line under the meter names the parts; hovering a part replaces it with that part's exact count. Each part's hover target is 32 px tall without moving the meter. Provider cache reads are not part of the meter: those tokens were in the context, only cheaper, and the card reports them on their own **Prompt cache** line. With no savings reported the meter is absent, never "0 saved". See `savingsMeter` in [`apps/nano/src/view_context.zig`](apps/nano/src/view_context.zig) and `savedSegments` in [`apps/nano/src/context_window.zig`](apps/nano/src/context_window.zig).

## Settings and account cards

Settings pages share `page`, `header`, `subtitle`, `card`, `row` and `toggle` from [`apps/nano/src/view_settings.zig`](apps/nano/src/view_settings.zig). The Accounts page ([`apps/nano/src/view_settings_accounts.zig`](apps/nano/src/view_settings_accounts.zig) and its `view_settings_accounts_*.zig` parts) groups logins by provider. Each group has a brand glyph and an Add account action; its rows show identity, active state, plan, usage windows, and account actions. A quiet device switcher in the header changes which host's local agent logins are shown.

Usage belongs **inside the existing account row**. Each window uses a short label, a 5 px rounded meter, a percentage, and a subdued reset time (`meter` in `view_settings_accounts_rows.zig`). It uses the theme accent below 80% used, warning at 80%, and danger at 95% (`apps/nano/src/accounts.zig`). Keep the provider ordering and card geometry stable as live values refresh; loading or unavailable data should not rearrange the page. The CodeGraff account section uses the same card geometry for credit balance, recent spending, and an optional monthly key-budget meter. It reads the selected device's Graff CLI account; Harness sync sign-in is a separate account state.

## Conversation and interaction

The conversation is the primary work surface. User messages and agent output have distinct alignment and hierarchy; tool calls, code, files, errors, and timestamps remain readable within the same transcript. The composer shows the selected agent, model, and reasoning level without hiding the prompt. Once a chat exists, its agent is fixed; model and reasoning changes stay within that agent. Popovers and dialogs use the same theme roles and compact controls as the rest of the shell. The context ring and percentage stay at the right of the composer footer, with token savings and detailed counts in the hover card. The context indicator stays muted until the warning (75%) and danger (90%) thresholds. This follows Phil Hedayatnia's composer work in the [hedayatnia/harness](https://github.com/hedayatnia/harness/tree/codex/dia-composer) fork.

The composer ([`apps/nano/src/view_composer.zig`](apps/nano/src/view_composer.zig)) is an opaque writing plane with **16 px** corners, a hairline edge, a large soft shadow, and a round send/stop button filled with the theme's primary accent, its glyph contrast-checked on that fill. Focus turns the edge to the accent at 55%. The compact composer is **49 px** tall; the new-session box starts at **104 px** and grows with the draft. Placeholder text uses the muted text role. The caret is a **1.5 px** hairline in the accent color (including the user's accent override), with 0.75 px rounded ends, inset 1 px (`message_caret`). The model switcher has a stable anchor at the bottom-left below the writing surface. Workspace controls follow it when space permits.

Use plain, specific copy for actions with data consequences. Local versus synced workspace state belongs in the account area. Importing existing chats into sync requires an explicit choice, and the interface should state when another signed-in device can request access to the host's workspace.

## Model picker

One composer chip opens one **356 px** card: agent tabs, a search row, and the model list ([`apps/nano/src/view_picker.zig`](apps/nano/src/view_picker.zig), rows from [`apps/nano/src/model_picker.zig`](apps/nano/src/model_picker.zig), modelled in `apps/nano/lean-proofs/Nano/ModelPicker.lean`). An existing chat offers only its own agent. A new chat with starred models opens on a **Starred** list that mixes agents. Rows are single lines: the model's mark, its name, a context badge when the catalog gives one, and a star. The selected row and the keyboard's row are lit, and hover moves the keyboard highlight.

Some agents, graff and opencode among them, serve models from several providers at once. On such a tab the rows sit under a heading per route, with the route's mark, its name in **11 px semibold** muted text, and a **Plan** badge when the route is included in the user's subscription. Headings are the same 34 px height as a row and are never highlighted or picked. Resting on a row shows a detail card beside it after **180 ms** (60 ms once one is showing); it stays while the pointer is over it and goes **160 ms** after the pointer leaves both. Clicking a row opens the model's page, with its reasoning on a slider (its ends read Faster and Deeper, or Off and On for a two-step ladder) and **Use model**.

## Tabs, split panes, and composer picks

⌘D splits the conversation column to the right, like Ghostty; ⌘T opens a chat tab (Settings → Shortcuts lists both; splitting downward has no default key). The focused pane is the selected chat with the composer; other panes show their chats read only, and clicking one moves focus there.

Each tab and pane owns its selection and composer picks (agent, model, reasoning). A new pane or tab starts from the picks of the pane it was opened from, with an empty prompt. Leaving a pane or tab parks its unsent prompt and picks, and coming back restores them. An existing chat's picks live on the chat itself. See `Draft` and `park` in [`apps/nano/src/tabs.zig`](apps/nano/src/tabs.zig).

## Generated-image galleries

Consecutive images in one reply share one card ([`apps/nano/src/view_image_gallery.zig`](apps/nano/src/view_image_gallery.zig)): the caption, an "N of M" count with Prev and Next, the selected image, and a strip of numbered thumbnails. Clicking the image opens the image preview. A single image renders on its own, and a matched before/after pair renders as a pair (`view_before_after.zig`).

## Image annotation

The image preview ([`apps/nano/src/view_image_preview.zig`](apps/nano/src/view_image_preview.zig)) has **Annotate** in its toolbar beside Close preview, Zoom out, Zoom in, Fit and Copy image. In annotate mode a click on the image drops a numbered pin, up to eight (`apps/nano/src/image_annotate.zig`); each pin gets a one-line note and a × to remove it. The stage keeps its height from the first pin to the eighth, so dropping a pin never moves the image under the pointer. The footer counts the pins and offers Undo, Clear and **Send feedback (N)**, which attaches the image and adds the numbered notes, with each pin's position as a percentage of the image, to the chat's draft. Esc leaves a note, then annotate mode, then the preview. Pin counts use tabular figures. See [`docs/annotate.md`](docs/annotate.md).

## Workspace modes

The desktop client has three workspace modes. They change how much of the workspace's chrome is on screen, not what a chat can do. The policy is modelled and proved in [`apps/nano/lean-proofs/Nano/UiMode3.lean`](apps/nano/lean-proofs/Nano/UiMode3.lean) (`profileOf`) and mirrored as `UiMode3` in [`apps/nano/src/ux_rules.zig`](apps/nano/src/ux_rules.zig); the running app's mode is `UiMode` in [`apps/nano/src/motion_rules.zig`](apps/nano/src/motion_rules.zig), with each mode's defaults in `HierarchyProfile.ofMode`.

- **Zen** is the default on a new install (`baseUiMode` in [`apps/nano/src/settings.zig`](apps/nano/src/settings.zig)). It is for calm, chat-first work: one flat session list, and no open-workspaces tree, project and status filters, agent and branch badges on rows, git and branch footer, prompt rail or actions bar. The right end of the title bar holds only the side-panel toggle. In a normal window with one chat open, the tab strip gives way and the title bar carries the chat's caption (`usesWindowedChatCaption` in `apps/nano/src/view_tab_strip_render.zig`).
- **Developer** is for running and reviewing work across projects. It shows all of the above, groups sessions by project, and adds the terminal and files toggles beside the side-panel toggle.
- **Design** keeps Zen's flat list and quiet chrome and adds the Design Canvas ([`apps/nano/src/view_design_canvas.zig`](apps/nano/src/view_design_canvas.zig), modelled in `DesignCanvas.lean`), which opens as a side-panel tab, with a button for it in the title bar.

When the title bar is short of room (under 420 px of chrome, or under 240 px in its last tab slot), Developer and Design fall back to the side-panel toggle alone, so tab labels are never clipped (`effectiveTrailingCount`). While Settings is open none of these buttons show.

Settings → Appearance has a Zen / Developer / Design control. Choosing a mode there, or from the command palette, applies its defaults, including the sidebar's grouping and row details (`applyUiMode`), and choosing Design opens the canvas. Settings also lists each part of the layout with its own switch; the chosen mode stays until every part matches the other of Zen and Developer, and Settings calls the layout customized and offers Reset. Two of the window's fixed shortcuts, which can't be rebound (`fixedWindowChords` in `KeymapPartition.lean`), change the mode. ⇧⌘M (Ctrl+Shift+M on Linux), like the View menu's **Toggle Zen / Developer Mode**, the mode button in the title bar and `/zen`, flips between Zen and Developer only; from Design it goes to Zen. ⌥⌘D (Ctrl+Alt+D on Windows) and `/design` switch Design on or off, opening or closing the canvas. The choice is saved as `uiMode` in the settings file.

Fullscreen (Ctrl+⌘F) changes the frame around the panes the same way in every mode ([`apps/nano/src/view_fullscreen_chrome.zig`](apps/nano/src/view_fullscreen_chrome.zig) does not read the mode). The sidebar closes and returns on exit, open menus and search close, and the window behind the panes is black. The tab strip and title bar give way to a pill at the top edge, which shows a floating bar with the tabs, a sidebar toggle and Exit full screen on a click or when the pointer rests at the edge, and to a left-edge peek that slides the sidebar in; Esc dismisses either. Each pane gets a 28 px caption strip above it with its chat, project, branch and device. The mode's own title-bar buttons (terminal, files, Design Canvas, side panel) are not drawn in fullscreen, and the windowed Zen chat caption is not used there.

## What every control and surface owes the user

These standards come from the product's issue history. A reviewer checks each one for any change that adds or alters a control or surface.

1. **It works.** Every visible, enabled control does something, and a saved setting is read somewhere that changes behaviour; a behaviour that isn't supported yet has no enabled control promising it (#115, #117, #119). *Check:* use each control in the change, and find the code outside its settings page that reads each key it writes.
2. **It says what is true.** A label, caption, tooltip and state describe what is actually the case (#121, #17, #85). *Check:* change the state underneath (mode, connection, download progress) and confirm the words change with it.
3. **One action, one control.** An action has one name, one icon and one place per surface, and the same control is not drawn twice on one screen. *Check:* look for the action's label and icon in the screenshot and in the code; each appears once per surface.
4. **Errors help.** An error names what failed, says why, and offers only a remedy that applies (#201, #125, #123, #44). *Check:* force each failure path in a test and read the message; a failed request is never silent, and a Retry is offered only where retrying can succeed.
5. **State stays put.** Drafts, attachments, model picks, scroll position and selection stay with their pane and chat across navigation (#84, #138, #141, #149, #150). *Check:* set each one, switch chat, tab and pane, restart, and come back.
6. **Text behaves natively.** Selection gestures, copy, and the system's selected-text interface work as they do elsewhere on the platform (#48, #49, #197, #22). *Check:* double-, triple- and four-click, drag across blocks, copy, and use the system's text services, in the transcript and the composer, in focused and unfocused panes.
7. **Markdown and math render.** Valid Markdown and math render, and valid input never falls back to raw source (#46, #91, #124, #127–#129, #153–#156). *Check:* add the input as a test case (math: `apps/nano/src/tests_native_math.zig`) and look at it rendered in a scene.
8. **Status is honest.** Never show success, "Done" or "Ready" for an outcome that is unknown or failed. *Check:* drive the failure and the unknown outcome (timeout, disconnect, quit mid-way) and confirm the final state says so.
9. **A window and fullscreen are one design.** A normal window and fullscreen show the same information once, the same controls and the same rhythm. *Check:* capture the change in both and compare them side by side.
10. **Evidence is synthetic and reproducible.** Tests and screenshots use made-up data, as "Synthetic data only" and "Every issue carries an image" in [`AGENTS.md`](AGENTS.md) require. *Check:* the pull request names the scene, fixture or test behind each image.

## iOS Home and iPhone Duo

The iOS session list reads like a messages list. A **27 pt** semibold "Sessions" title, then a pinned row of agent avatars (only while something is pinned and no search is active), then the filter chips. Every row starts with the session's agent as a 44 pt avatar, then the name at **16 pt** semibold, the "space @ device" line at **14 pt**, and the branch, times and status words at **12 pt**. Two weights only, regular and semibold. Times use tabular figures. Content sits on a **24 pt** side gutter with no row dividers. Chips are 36 pt capsules on Liquid Glass with a faint ink fill, a little deeper when changed from the default, never a solid fill; their hit area stays 44 pt. A finished run with an unread reply shows its time and a dot in the accent color; red stays for failures. The pressed and selected row wash uses an 18 pt continuous radius, concentric with the avatar it surrounds. See `ChatRow`, `PinnedRow` and `HomeControlPill` in `apps/ios`.

Layout follows size classes and the space the window has, since on iPhone Duo and in iPhone Mirroring an iPhone app can have any combination. Regular width and height on iPad shows the list beside the open session. iPhone Duo's inner display (an iPhone window regular both ways) lying flat is the ordinary one-column iPhone app; only folded like a book does it put the list and the session on either side of the fold. See `HomeColumns`. Regular width with compact height (a Max in landscape) can also show two sessions side by side. Compact width keeps the single stack or the opt-in phone split. On iPhone Duo, an active fold (`.division` reserved region) puts the list and the session on either side of it in book pose and stacks them in laptop pose, and active `.occlusion` regions push content clear. See `apps/ios/Harness/Theme/FoldLayout.swift`.

## Status and tag chips

Status pills, tags and badges (Working, Needs you, Failed, Queued, a pull request number, a "git" tag) use a soft tint of their hue behind darker, saturated text in that same hue. Never a solid saturated fill with white text: it is harder to read and shouts over the row it labels. On iOS the tint is the hue at **12%** in light and **16%** in dark, and the text is the hue mixed **35%** toward black in light or **20%** toward white in dark. With those values every status hue on both Codegraff appearances (danger, warning, success, accent) measures at least **4.5:1** against its tinted chip. No border on the chip. Use `tintedChip(_:)` / `StatusChip` in `apps/ios`, and recheck contrast when adding a hue or a theme. Reference: [@marcelkargul](https://x.com/marcelkargul/status/2115949035009814905).

## Extending the design

Reuse the semantic `theme.Colors` roles, the `view_kit.zig` helpers, the shared settings widgets in `view_settings.zig`, existing icons, and the current account-row layout. Check both Codegraff appearances and a contrasting third-party theme, in each workspace mode and in fullscreen. Review ordinary, hover, focus, loading, offline, error, and high-usage states, and the standards in [What every control and surface owes the user](#what-every-control-and-surface-owes-the-user). [`docs/theme-system.md`](docs/theme-system.md) lists the theme validation and visual review surfaces.
